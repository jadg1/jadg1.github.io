01 / Networks Complexity Science Hub Vienna · Current

Can a Generative Model Learn How World Trade Rewires Itself?

  • Applied GenDeg, a normalizing-flow generative model for dynamic graphs, to 30 years of CEPII BACI trade data: 172 countries, 96 product sectors, 1995 to 2024
  • Built the 15-stage training and evaluation pipeline, and benchmarked the model against gravity models, latent-space and STERGM baselines, and deliberately stripped-down nulls
  • The answer is partly yes and partly no, and one of the affirmative results turned out to be a statistical artefact that I found and killed
Supervised by Leonardo N. Ialongo
Python, PyTorch, Hydra, polars · 2026
A hole in the standard class, in three nodes
Edge-independent models · exclusivity · co-occurrence
Erdős-Rényi, Chung-Lu, stochastic block models and all four gravity models used in trade economics belong to one class: every possible link gets its own probability and its own independent coin flip. That class has a hole, and three countries are enough to show it.
  1. Exclusivity: the variance it cannot reach +
    A degree in this class is a sum of independent coin flips, so its variance is pinned by the marginals. The model cannot represent a country whose number of partners is more certain than its choice of partner.
  2. Co-occurrence: an impossibility, not a bad fit +
    Where all three links exist or none, expected triangles are 0.500 and the best independent fit gives 0.125. Chanpuriya et al. cap any edge-independent model at 0.306 unless it partially memorises the graph and stops generating variety.
  3. One hole seen from both sides +
    Carrying only marginals means having neither the negative dependence that pins a country's partner count nor the positive dependence that closes a triangle. Diversity and structure trade off inside the class, and the theorem says by how much.
  4. How GenDeg escapes by construction +
    It models the count first, next year's generalized degree, and only then places edges, so a degree is never a sum of independent Bernoullis. Whether that theoretical escape shows up on real trade data is the empirical question.
The shape of persistence, not its level
Impulse response to a shock in a country's partner count
Shock a country's number of trade partners and watch the effect decay. Observed trade decays gradually, and so does GenDeg. The gravity response collapses at horizon 1, which is independent sampling noise disappearing, then flattens into a tail driven purely by GDP. It fails in the same direction in 10 of 10 sectors, on a feature no refitting can reach.
  1. Right decay shape, slightly too persistent +
    GenDeg reaches persistence 0.842 against an observed 0.814, running above in 6 of 10 sectors. The gravity family sits at 0.711 to 0.721 and below observed in 10 of 10.
  2. Most persistence is covariates, not dynamics +
    A model with no dynamics at all, each year an independent cross-sectional fit, still recovers about 73% of cumulative degree persistence. That reframes the target: the interesting quantity was never the level, it was the shape.
  3. Two failure modes, reality in between +
    Models with an explicit carry-forward term overshoot (GenDeg 1.21×, STERGM 1.34×); models that redraw edges each step undershoot (latent-space 0.40×). No simple generator lands on reality. Over-persistence is a property of a class, not a quirk.
  4. Rate right, mechanism wrong +
    Left running free, mean degree error stays under 1.6% for four years while the degree Gini falls from 0.591 to 0.422 against an observed 0.595. Reality adds partners while holding the hierarchy; GenDeg fills the bottom and erodes the top.
  5. Blind to what is being traded +
    Observed persistence rises across the Rauch bands from 0.624 to 0.685 as theory predicts. GenDeg is flat to three decimals. A purely topological generator has no channel through which product type can matter.
The result I killed
A binning artefact, and what survived it
My earlier headline was that trade-link retention is far more over-dispersed than independent retention allows, evidence of exactly the edge dependence the toys say the standard class cannot represent. It was largely an artefact of how the benchmark was binned, and I found it in my own result.
  1. A clean process read 3.9 instead of 1.0
    The benchmark was built from each bin's mean degree, so within-bin degree variation contributed a term it could not see. A homogeneous, perfectly independent simulated process scored 3.9. My toy validations missed it because they calibrate at exact degree, where that term is identically zero.
  2. After the fix, no residual is left to explain
    Simulating the null on the same degrees rather than the bin mean, the edge-independent family brackets the observed value: Poisson 1.04, observed 1.18, negative binomial 1.29. On this statistic the data give no evidence that trade retention requires edge dependence.
  3. The persistence result was untouched
    F1 uses a panel VAR on degree series with no binning and re-ran identical to the last digit. It now carries more of the argument than it did before, and the toys still do the theoretical work on their own.
The takeaway. The toys prove the standard class has a representational hole. The data, once the benchmark was fixed, do not show that trade needs it. Those are different claims and the project keeps them apart.
02 / Banking MSc Finance thesis · UPF BSM

Does Cross-Border Common Ownership Diversify Bank Risk?

  • Tests whether shared institutional shareholders transmit systemic risk between European banks, a contagion channel the macroprudential framework barely touches
  • Distinguishes cross-border from domestic common ownership in the EU banking sector, a separation absent from prior work
  • Speaks directly to the live Banking Union debate on integration vs. contagion
Supervised by Albert Banal-Estañol
Completed with merit · 2026
The contagion channel nobody is monitoring
EU banks · cross-border vs domestic common ownership
Banking Union supervision tracks lending and interbank exposures. But two banks in different countries can also be linked by a third party, a large institutional investor that owns meaningful stakes in both. If that investor reallocates, both banks move together.
What's new
An empirical separation prior work does not make
Existing studies treat institutional ownership as one number. We split it into the part that crosses borders and the part that stays at home, and ask whether the geography of ownership matters for systemic risk.
  1. Cross-border vs domestic ownership +
    Splits common ownership by investor geography rather than treating it as a single aggregate.
  2. European banking sector +
    Brings the common-ownership literature into the Banking Union debate, where the geographic dimension is central.
  3. Supervisory perimeter test +
    Tests whether the EBA boundary marks a meaningful economic frontier for the contagion channel.
  4. Ownership-aware integration +
    Re-frames financial integration around shareholders, not just lending and interbank exposures.
Why it matters for policy
Implications for macroprudential supervision in the EU
The Buch position, deepen integration but manage contagion, is broadly supported. But contagion need not arrive only through the channels currently monitored. Shared shareholders are a parallel route the macroprudential framework barely covers.
  1. Ownership transparency belongs in the macroprudential toolkit
    Supervisors already track lending and capital. The investor side of the balance sheet deserves the same visibility.
  2. Stress tests should include common-investor scenarios
    Standard scenarios capture asset shocks. They should also capture coordinated reallocation by shared shareholders.
  3. Banking Union integration should be ownership-aware
    The supervisory perimeter alone may not capture where contagion travels. Integration policy needs to look through to the investor base.
The takeaway. Integration is desirable; contagion is the side-effect. Watching only the lending side leaves a blind spot on the equity side.
03 / Trade MSc Quantitative Economics thesis · UAM

Derisking European Critical Raw Material Dependencies with Graph Learning

  • Combines structural trade economics with graph machine learning to study the EU's exposure to critical raw materials
  • Asks where the EU sources strategic materials, and what happens if a major supplier becomes unavailable
  • Produces a risk taxonomy aligned with the EU 2030 Critical Raw Materials Act
Supervised by Santiago Pérez Balsalobre and Javier Bas
Graded 9/10 with Excellence · now a working paper · 2026
A structural mismatch in EU sourcing
Where strategic dependencies actually sit
Trade theory predicts that countries source most from partners that are nearby, culturally close, and politically aligned. For the materials the EU's green and digital transitions depend on, the pattern is roughly the opposite.
What's new
Two literatures, one question
Trade economics tells us why patterns hold. Graph learning tells us what happens when they break. The thesis combines the two so policy questions about EU material security can be answered with both.
  1. Structural trade + graph learning +
    Brings dynamic graph neural networks into a domain dominated by gravity models, without abandoning the structural intuition.
  2. Trade as an evolving network +
    Treats the global CRM trade system as a dynamic graph rather than a stack of bilateral flows, making relational structure first-class.
  3. Supplier-disruption scenarios +
    Asks what concentration looks like if a major supplier becomes unavailable. That is the question policy actually has to answer.
  4. Policy-aligned risk taxonomy +
    Translates model outputs into a classification compatible with the EU's own concentration thresholds.
Why it matters for policy
A quantitative complement to the EU 2030 Critical Raw Materials agenda
The EU has set diversification, processing, and recycling targets for the materials its green and digital transitions depend on. The thesis offers a way to prioritize those targets, telling policymakers where the structural exposure is largest before the next disruption forces the answer.
  1. Identify which materials carry the most structural risk
    Move the conversation from "all 16 are critical" to a defensible ranking of where attention is most needed first.
  2. Stress-test dependencies before they break
    Quantify what supplier-by-supplier disruption would do to market concentration, in advance of the political moment that makes it urgent.
  3. Frame de-risking as a structural problem
    EU exposure is not random. It sits with distant, politically misaligned partners. De-risking has to confront the geography, not just the volumes.
The takeaway. Resource security is not a list of materials. It's a network with structural weak points, and those weak points can be located before they fail.
04 / Deep Learning IEEE CBMS 2026 · In press

A Model Too Small to Memorise the Patient

  • A convolutional network for medical image analysis operating on 404 to 1,039 trainable parameters, where conventional architectures use millions
  • Convolutional multi-head attention captures global spatial structure without adding depth, so relational processing substitutes for parameter redundancy
  • Benchmarked across twelve independent clinical datasets, matching models up to 29,000 times larger in parameter count
With F. Félix Arredondo, N. Nieto Gutiérrez, G. De Los Ríos Alatorre, E. Gallareta Flores and L. A. Muñoz Ubando
39th IEEE International Symposium on Computer-Based Medical Systems · 2026
Smallness as a design principle
Four orders of magnitude · comparable accuracy
Compression is usually treated as a cost of deployment, something you accept to fit a model on a device. Here the parameter budget is the design itself. A network that cannot memorise its training set has different properties, not merely a smaller footprint.
What's new
Relational processing in place of parameter redundancy
Existing compact medical models still count parameters in the millions, or specialise to a single imaging modality. MicroClinic holds an ultra-low regime below 1.1k across twelve datasets, and stays small enough that the learned representation can be inspected rather than only measured.
  1. Convolutional multi-head attention +
    Global spatial dependencies are captured through attention rather than by stacking layers, so receptive field grows without the depth that drives parameter count.
  2. An ultra-low regime that holds +
    404 parameters for binary tasks and 1,039 for multi-class, against prior compact work that remains between 0.4M and 1.1M or is tailored to one modality.
  3. Twelve datasets, not one +
    Benchmarked across independent clinical datasets rather than tuned to a single benchmark, reaching 99.9% on MedicalMNIST and 95.7% on COVID-19 X-ray.
  4. Representations you can analyse +
    The reduced scale makes structural analysis tractable through Fisher discriminant ratio and mutual information, turning interpretability into a measurement rather than a post hoc story.
Why it matters
Privacy and deployability as architectural properties
As parameter counts grow, networks memorise training samples, and recoverable information becomes encoded in the weights. In clinical settings that is a vulnerability, not an inefficiency. A model with almost no capacity to memorise cannot leak what it never stored.
  1. A narrower surface for inference attacks
    Membership inference and reconstruction attacks exploit memorised training data. Limiting capacity limits what such an attack can recover.
  2. Screening where the hardware is old
    A footprint deployable on machines with 8 to 16 MB of RAM removes the infrastructure barrier that keeps AI-assisted screening out of underserved and legacy clinical environments.
  3. Interpretability that regulators can ask for
    Clinical adoption depends on transparent reasoning, and obligations under emerging AI regulation point the same way. Small models make that a property of the design rather than an add-on.
The takeaway. Privacy is usually promised in a policy and defended by process. Here it is a consequence of the architecture: the model has nowhere to keep the patient.
05 / Systems Founders Hackathon Hamburg · Team AI Beavers

A Payment Rail for Machine Clients

  • Go HTTP gateway that holds automated clients behind a machine-readable 402 challenge until payment is verified. No content leaks before the grant
  • Companion MCP server acts as the agent-side wallet: budget check, payment, and grant polling as three tools an autonomous client can call
  • EU data residency published as a live, machine-verifiable endpoint, backed by an append-only audit ledger
Go · Ed25519-signed JWT grants · Mollie · MCP
Hackathon build · 2026 · code
Charge the crawler instead of blocking it
HTTP 402, made parseable
Publishers today choose between blocking automated clients and giving the content away. A third option exists if the refusal is legible to a machine: answer with a structured 402 that states the price and the retry path, and let the agent settle it and come back.
What was built
Gateway, wallet, grants, ledger
Protocol design rather than a prototype UI: a challenge format a client can parse, a signed credential it can present, and a record of every decision the gateway made.
  1. Machine-readable 402 +
    The refusal carries a structured body and a payment header stating price, accepted methods and retry path, parseable without a human in the loop.
  2. Ed25519-signed access grants +
    Grants are signed JWTs scoped to a resource path, with TTL and per-token quota. The public key is served so verification can happen at the edge.
  3. Wallet as an MCP server +
    Three tools give an autonomous client a spending identity: check the remaining daily budget, execute the payment, poll until the grant is ready. Larger amounts require explicit approval.
  4. Provider-agnostic settlement +
    A mock provider for local runs and Mollie for real test-mode transactions, switched by configuration rather than code.
Why it matters
Sovereignty as an implemented property
European digital sovereignty is usually argued for in documents. Here it is a running endpoint: the gateway states its processing region, its sub-processors, and that no data crosses borders, and a machine can check the claim.
  1. A commercial answer to agent traffic
    As more requests come from autonomous clients, publishers need something between blanket blocking and free access. A payable refusal is that something.
  2. Residency you can verify, not just declare
    Region, sub-processors and cross-border transfer status are served at a well-known endpoint, so the compliance claim is testable by the party relying on it.
  3. Auditability by construction
    Every access decision and grant issuance lands in an append-only ledger: the record supervision and oversight actually need, produced as a side-effect of normal operation.
The takeaway. Rules for machine clients are easier to defend when they are enforceable in the protocol rather than asserted in the terms of service.
